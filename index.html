Why Wednesday’s rally paid about 3.7×, and Thursday’s drop only cost about 2×

The June 2027 $225 call is a long-dated, in-the-money option. Its built-in leverage is roughly delta × stock/option, or about 3.5–4×. That showed up cleanly on Wednesday, when Amazon jumped after investors re-rated AWS and the midterms. It did not show up symmetrically on Thursday, because implied volatility rose as the stock sold off and cushioned the call.

Does the 6–7% / 20–25% vs 2–3% / 4–5% framing hold?

Yes, if you mark both days against the prior close — which is how an existing position would feel.

  • Wednesday: Amazon was officially +6.12% on the day and +6.70% by noon versus Tuesday’s $250.69 close. A modeled mark on the 225 call rose about 22.5% by the close and 25.0% by noon (leverage 3.7×). That matches “up 20–25%, about 3.5–4×.” Most of that stock move was an overnight gap: the cash open was already $264.78, up 5.6%. From the 9:30 open to noon the stock only added another 1.0%.
  • Thursday: Versus Wednesday’s $266.03 close, Amazon was −1.83% at noon and −2.19% at the $260.21 official close. The modeled call was −3.3% at noon and −4.5% at the close — right on “down about 4–5%.” That is not the whole morning, though. The stock opened up at $271.64, washed out to $252.27 around 10:50 (−5.2% vs Wednesday, −7.1% vs the open), then bounced. At that low the modeled call was down about 14%, still better than the 18% a constant-IV model would have printed.

Thursday, November 5

Amazon gapped up again — a Prime / Uber Eats tie-up hit the tape — then reversed as a broad tech selloff took over. Democrats’ midterm wins revived fears of tighter AI rules and pauses on data-center construction. The Nasdaq dropped about 5.5%. Amazon’s official session ran $271.43–$273.23 down to $252.27 and closed at $260.21, down 2.19%.

Volatility flipped hard the other way. The VIX jumped as much as 5.7 points to above 28 about 90 minutes after the open (around the same time Amazon printed its low) and closed at 28.18, up about 70% — its biggest one-day rise since April. Amazon’s 30-day ATM IV went from about 30% early, with the stock near $271, to 37.6% later in the day. Longer-dated IV moved less, as it should: the April 2027 ATM tenor rose about 3.2 vol points. The model gives this June 2027 225 strike a +3.5 vol-point spike at the low, with most of that increase sticking into the bounce.

The math: delta leverage plus a vega offset

leverage ≈ (% call move) / (% stock move) ≈ delta × S / C

At Tuesday’s close the model has delta 0.72 on a $47.64 call with Amazon at $250.69, so 0.72 × 250.69 / 47.64 ≈ 3.8×. That is the structural reason Wednesday paid about 3.5–4×. Gamma added a little extra on the way up; a modest IV crush subtracted a little.

A 1-vol-point change is worth about $0.60–$0.66 of call value here (vega). A 3-point IV rise is therefore worth roughly $1.80–$2.00 — enough to cancel a large piece of a 2% downside delta hit, but not enough to cancel a 5% washout.

Plain-English takeaway

This call is not a 1-for-1 bet on Amazon. Because it already has a lot of intrinsic value, a dollar in the stock moves the call by about 75 cents — but that 75 cents is a much bigger percentage of the call premium than of the stock. That is the 3.5–4×. It works in both directions if volatility sits still.

Volatility did not sit still. Wednesday, index and single-name IV fell as the election passed; that shaved a little off the call’s gain but the delta/gamma from a 6% gap-up still delivered a roughly 23–25% rise. Thursday, IV jumped as tech sold off. The extra premium from that spike replaced a large share of what delta was taking away, so a 2% decline in Amazon only showed up as a 4–5% decline in the call — as long as you mark it to the prior close and not to the $252 washout.

The asymmetry is not magic and it is not guaranteed. It is vega. If IV had not risen Thursday, the same 2.2% stock drop would have been about an 8% hit on the call, much closer to the 3.6× starting leverage.
